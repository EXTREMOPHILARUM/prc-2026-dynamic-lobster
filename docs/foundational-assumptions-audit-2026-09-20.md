# Foundational assumptions audit - 2026-09-20

This document audits the premises used to make modelling, validation, compliance, and
prioritisation decisions in this repository. It is the canonical correction layer over the
historical experiment record. Historical documents are intentionally preserved, but a claim
listed here as contradicted, overstated, unresolved, or stale must not be used as a current
decision premise without new evidence.

The audit used:

- the raw 2025 training, 2026 ranking, and submission parquet files;
- saved out-of-fold prediction artifacts under `data/interim`;
- the production v29 model metadata and scoring path;
- experiment, rehearsal, robustness, bootstrap, and analysis scripts;
- the saved official challenge pages under `docs/reference`;
- the project policy in `docs/rules-and-compliance.md`.

No leaderboard delta was used to calculate or select a model parameter in this audit.

## Executive verdict

The repository's primary data understanding is sound:

- the departure target is exactly `MVT_TIME_UTC_mvt - BLOCK_TIME_UTC_mvt`;
- arrivals store taxi-in under the same target column and must not be used as departure labels;
- Network Manager clocks, especially `AOBT_3_flt`, are the dominant supplied predictor;
- departures without a complete NM record are rare but contain a disproportionate share of
  squared error;
- extreme targets must remain in validation;
- complete-month temporal folds are preferable to random splits;
- exact flight and callsign categories have severe July 2026 transfer problems.

However, several current strategic conclusions do not have the same evidential quality. The
most important problems are:

1. The stored analysis called "v29 OOF" is an approximation rather than an exact OOF
   reconstruction of production v29.
2. The stacker's reported OOF gain is not strictly nested.
3. January and July 2025 have repeatedly been used for selection and are no longer independent
   confirmation months.
4. Production v29 uses finalized same-day EUROCONTROL aggregates whose prediction-time
   availability has not been established.
5. Leaderboard observations have influenced thresholds, confidence, causal interpretations,
   and priorities despite the project's no-leaderboard-selection policy.
6. The drop-1/drop-10/drop-50 diagnostic has been elevated into a universal rejection rule,
   although a predeclared small-cohort mechanism can be concentrated by construction.
7. The row bootstrap does not account for airport-day, weather-event, or temporal dependence.
8. Claims that the remaining error is irreducible, that the honest ceiling has been reached,
   or that roughly 0.2% of rows are broken clocks are stronger than the evidence.
9. ADS-B geometry/context features remain unresolved, while target-flight post-event use also
   remains blocked by the project's conservative temporal-availability policy.
10. Several current-facing documents retain stale or mutually contradictory instructions.

## Evidence labels

This audit uses five labels:

- **Verified**: independently reproduced from raw data, code, or saved artifacts.
- **Contradicted**: directly falsified by data, code, or a later controlled experiment.
- **Overstated**: evidence supports a narrower statement than the one documented.
- **Unresolved**: available evidence cannot settle the claim.
- **Stale**: historically accurate or useful, but superseded and unsafe as current guidance.

## High-impact findings

### 1. Production v29 does not have an exact stored OOF reconstruction

**Status: Contradicted as currently described.**

`scripts/analyse_v29_layers.py:35-52` reconstructs the analysed chain from:

- `v21_oof_predictions.parquet`;
- one `catgpu_d8` AOBT member, given total weight 0.3;
- one LOBT member, given weight 0.3;
- the Rome mixture;
- the stored stacker correction.

Production v29 instead has `use_atfm: true`, two separate AOBT CatBoost members at weight 0.15
each, one LOBT member at weight 0.3, and the stacker, as recorded in
`models/mechanism_v29.json:144-161`.

No v26-equivalent OOF artifact was found. Consequently the documented 307.27 January/July RMSE,
layer contribution table, residual anatomy, signed-anchor shares, and ceiling calculations are
measurements of a close reconstruction, not the deployed v29 chain.

**Safe conclusion:** the stored reconstruction is useful for diagnosis, but all references to
it should say "v29 approximation" until every production component has nested OOF predictions.

### 2. Stacker validation is not strictly nested

**Status: Methodologically contaminated.**

`scripts/experiment_stacker.py:55-60` loads stored component OOF predictions. The stacker then
holds out month `m` and trains on the other eleven months at `scripts/experiment_stacker.py:84-87`.

For a meta-training row in month `k`, its component prediction was generated by a component model
that held out `k` but generally trained on month `m`. Therefore the stacker's meta-training
features indirectly depend on the outer validation month's labels. The validation-month component
prediction itself is out of fold, but the meta-model training set is not independent of that
month.

This matters because the claimed stacker gain is approximately 0.7-0.8 seconds. Production
stacking on all OOF rows is normal; the problem is the strength of the validation claim.

**Required correction:** regenerate every component prediction inside each stacker outer fold, or
describe the existing number as a non-nested screen rather than confirmation.

### 3. January and July are selection months, not untouched holdouts

**Status: Overstated validation evidence.**

January and July 2025 are calendar-matched to the ranking months, but they have been repeatedly
examined and used to choose or retain:

- LIRF band edges in `src/prc/lirfbands.py:28-33`;
- deterministic Rome rules in `src/prc/lirfbands.py:141-157`;
- stacker architecture and round count in `scripts/experiment_stacker.py:78-92`;
- robustness verdicts in `scripts/robustness_check.py:66-116`;
- multiple model scopes, blends, and acceptance decisions recorded in the experiment log.

Using these labels is permitted because they are 2025 training labels. The issue is statistical,
not a competition-rule violation. Their results are no longer unbiased confirmation evidence.

**Safe conclusion:** continue reporting January and July separately, but label them
calendar-matched selection diagnostics. Confirmation requires nested predeclared tests, untouched
months, or another year.

### 4. Same-day ATFM features may contain future context

**Status: Unresolved compliance and deployment risk.**

`src/prc/atfm.py:20-30` joins finalized daily airport aggregates using the UTC date of each
departure. Features include:

- regulated share;
- late-out share;
- arrival ATFM delay per flight;
- weather ATFM minutes;
- daily movement total.

For a morning departure, a finalized same-day aggregate can include operations after that
departure. Production v29 enables these features at `models/mechanism_v29.json:145`.

The organiser approved the dataset as open. That ruling does not establish that finalized daily
values were available before each prediction event. The project's own external-data checklist
requires this to be documented in `docs/rules-and-compliance.md:126-139`.

**Required correction:** obtain a temporal-availability ruling, establish that retrospective
full-day context is permitted, or remove/lag these features in the prize-eligible model.

### 5. Leaderboard evidence has affected decisions

**Status: Conflicts with project policy.**

The policy prohibits using leaderboard changes as validation observations or to select features,
thresholds, calibration, blends, row corrections, or priorities
(`docs/rules-and-compliance.md:48-59`). The following uses go beyond passive recording:

- `docs/experiment-log.md:1114-1118` invokes three public teams' board results to override a
  robustness failure for the LIRF band mechanism.
- `docs/experiment-log.md:1356-1361` interprets v25's unexpectedly large board gain as evidence
  that the 4-8 hour band is denser in 2026.
- `docs/experiment-log.md:1500-1504` and `docs/state.md:130-135` derive an approximately
  0.3-second future acceptance threshold from v28-v30 board transfer.
- `docs/state.md:136-139` uses v30's board reversal as evidence for the robustness gate.
- `docs/submissions.md:351-373` calls the transfer ledger descriptive, then uses it to impose
  requirements on future candidates.
- `docs/handoff-2026-09-20.md:59-62,214-218` uses a rival's leaderboard stage attribution to
  make ADS-B the main strategic priority.

**Safe conclusion:** leaderboard outcomes can remain in the submission history. They must not set
future acceptance thresholds, causal explanations, parameter values, or priorities.

### 6. The drop-row check is a diagnostic, not a universal rejection rule

**Status: Overstated.**

`scripts/robustness_check.py:98-116` declares a candidate fragile or weak when gains disappear
after removing the rows helped most. This correctly detects accidental dependence on arbitrary
extreme rows.

It is not universally valid. A predeclared observable mechanism with only a few eligible rows is
necessarily concentrated. The v15 LIRF band mechanism failed deep row removal yet delivered the
largest model improvement in the project. The experiment log itself recognises this at
`docs/experiment-log.md:1114-1118`.

The leaderboard result must not be used to validate the exception, but the logical distinction
exists independently:

- accidental concentration after searching a broad candidate family is a warning;
- concentration on the entire support of a predeclared small cohort is expected and calls for
  uncertainty bounds, not automatic rejection.

**Required correction:** report cohort size, predeclaration status, leave-one-month-out stability,
and clustered uncertainty alongside drop-row results. Do not let `verdict()` make the final
scientific decision.

### 7. The paired bootstrap is conditional and too optimistic for transfer claims

**Status: Useful but overstated.**

`scripts/paired_bootstrap.py:59-70` resamples individual rows within month strata. This preserves
the candidate/baseline pairing and is useful for detecting row-level instability in the observed
sample.

It does not account for dependence among departures sharing:

- an airport and day;
- a weather system;
- a disruption or feed outage;
- neighbouring-flight context features;
- a rare reporting event.

It also does not represent candidate-selection uncertainty, model refitting, month-to-month
variation, or year transfer. A row-level confidence interval excluding zero is not proof that a
gain generalises to 2026.

**Required correction:** add airport-day or date-block resampling and report both row and cluster
intervals. Treat either interval as conditional on the observed 2025 months.

### 8. The "0.2% broken clocks" narrative is not established

**Status: Contradicted in its broad form.**

`docs/state.md:25-30` and `docs/handoff-2026-09-20.md:23-28` describe roughly 0.2% of departures
as broken clocks. The evidence does establish severe timestamp anomalies, especially date slips
and some schedule-copy regimes. It does not establish that every long row, or 0.2% of all rows,
is corrupted.

Later evidence shows:

- the 199,634-row "schedule fallback" story was wrong; most cases are ordinary near-schedule
  pushbacks (`docs/experiment-log.md:75-91`);
- the genuine exact-copy excess is approximately 0.15% per candidate source, not the earlier
  1.18% raw match rate (`docs/experiment-log.md:590-600`);
- many long "neither" rows are genuine long taxi observations under a different off-block
  reporting definition (`docs/experiment-log.md:852-897`).

**Safe conclusion:** a small number of severe reporting anomalies dominate some folds. Their
total prevalence and the boundary between corruption and legitimate clock-definition disagreement
remain uncertain.

### 9. "Observability failure" and "irreducible clock noise" exceed the evidence

**Status: Overstated.**

The early-anchor experiments in `docs/experiment-log.md:1682-1711` show that the tested
probability-weighted residual experts lose overall, even with live-chain signals. Sections 34-36
show that several physical and grouped-mean corrections add no robust residual gain.

This supports:

> No tested score-time observable and model family has extracted a robust correction from the
> remaining early-anchor and ordinary matched residuals.

It does not prove:

- absence of information in every supplied covariate;
- irreducible Bayes error;
- impossibility for another estimator or representation;
- a definitive honest-model ceiling.

The stronger statements in `docs/handoff-2026-09-20.md:298-335` and
`docs/experiment-log.md:1827-1867` should be treated as hypotheses supported by repeated negative
results, not established facts.

### 10. ADS-B is unresolved and temporally blocked

**Status: Unresolved.**

The original coverage argument used one date, `2026-07-15`. Section 37 explicitly retracts the
0%/8%/0% LIRF/LFPG/LTFM bound because same-date coverage varies from 0% to 97% across years and
reflects feed/ingest behavior (`docs/experiment-log.md:1869-1906`).

What is verified:

- the current trace-derived off-block heuristic is much worse than NM AOBT;
- ADS-B usually starts after true off-block in the available joined sample;
- the local extractor does not reproduce the rival's unpublished geometry implementation.

What is not verified locally:

- representative 2026 January/July coverage;
- the value of coverage codes and stand geometry against the early-anchor residual;
- the rival's approximately nine-second stage attribution;
- compliance of target-flight post-event trajectories with the prediction-time rule.

`docs/rules-and-compliance.md:61-65` currently prohibits joining a ranking flight to its own
post-event ADS-B until the organiser clarifies the prediction-time cutoff. Licence approval alone
does not answer this.

**Required correction:** obtain the temporal ruling before modelling target-flight trajectories.
If permitted, perform a multi-date coverage census before a feature build.

### 11. Absolute LOMO-to-board ceiling calculations are not valid foundations

**Status: Overstated and population-mismatched.**

The repository correctly notes that leave-one-month-out models have never seen the held-out
season, while the production model trains on all 2025 seasons. `docs/experiment-log.md:922-926`
therefore says not to compare absolute LOMO RMSE directly with the full-data board score.

Nevertheless:

- `docs/experiment-log.md:568-571` scales a perfect-tail local score by the ratio of board to
  local RMSE;
- `docs/state.md:52-57` compares a local score after removing two rows with the board to infer
  that the hidden set carries equivalent unpredictable rows;
- `docs/tail-ceiling-audit.md:349-366` uses the same style of scaling for hypothetical scored
  outcomes.

These comparisons mix model training regimes, years, seasonal withholding, feature distributions,
and unknown target draws. They can illustrate sensitivity but cannot identify the hidden error
composition or establish a board ceiling.

### 12. The prize threshold is mislabeled

**Status: Contradicted.**

The official saved overview says the combined prize is for the first three teams
(`docs/reference/index.html:3060`). `docs/state.md:16` and
`docs/handoff-2026-09-20.md:18-21` call rank 4 the prize cutoff.

To enter the prize positions, the relevant threshold is the current third-place score. Rank 4 can
describe the first team outside the prize positions, but it is not itself the score to beat unless
the third and fourth scores are tied.

### 13. Scorer-result provenance is incomplete locally

**Status: Protocol gap.**

`docs/rules-and-compliance.md:80-85` requires preserving the scorer result JSON with local
submission provenance. `docs/submissions.md:1-5` says the scores came from bucket result JSONs.
No scorer result JSON was found in the workspace. Submission metadata records model provenance but
does not preserve the exact scorer response or its hash.

**Required correction:** download and preserve every available result JSON, or record that exact
historical recovery is unavailable. Future submissions should save the response automatically.

### 14. Reproducibility depends on untracked artifacts

**Status: Documented limitation, but understated in reproduction claims.**

The current analysis scripts read precomputed files from git-ignored `data/interim` and model
files from git-ignored `models`. For example:

- `scripts/analyse_error_anatomy.py` reads several stored OOF artifacts;
- `scripts/analyse_v29_layers.py:31-40` reads five component artifacts;
- `.gitignore` excludes `data/`, `models/`, and `submissions/`.

The quoted analysis commands reproduce results on this workstation because the artifacts are
present. They do not reproduce them from a fresh checkout. There is no complete manifest mapping
every v29 OOF component to its generation command, source commit, input hashes, and model metadata.

### 15. Rival-derived evidence is motivational, not verified evidence

**Status: Unresolved.**

The handoff relies on a rival's ADS-B stage ledger, coverage codes, early-row recovery rates, and
MSE attribution in `docs/handoff-2026-09-20.md:53-85`. The same passage states that the relevant
extractor and feature table are not public. No pinned source, archived feature table, or independent
local reproduction exists here.

These claims can motivate a local experiment. They cannot establish observability, coverage, or a
project ceiling.

## Smaller factual discrepancies

### Two-hour prevalence comment

`src/prc/features.py:154-155` says approximately 0.07% of rows exceed two hours. The raw value is:

```text
584 / 2,085,047 = 0.028009%
```

The accompanying squared-error share is model-dependent. Under the v29 approximation these rows
carry 29.373256% of SSE; they carry 41.720773% of variance around the global target mean.

### Arrival count wording

There are 2,082,750 arrival movements, but only 2,082,749 have a non-null target.

### NM descriptor wording

The four core off-block clocks share an exact null mask. Some associated descriptors have extra
nulls, and `FLIGHT_ID_mvt` remains populated on a few no-record rows. "The complete NM record is
absent" is operationally useful but should not be read as every `_flt` field having an identical
null mask.

### Model-version-specific error shares

Tail and no-record SSE shares changed materially between v11/v12 and the v29 approximation. Both
sets of numbers can be correct for their model versions. Every such statistic must name its exact
OOF artifact and model reconstruction.

## Safeguards that are correctly implemented

The audit found the following controls working as intended:

- training and evaluation filter to departures and retain extreme targets;
- primary component CV holds out complete calendar months;
- held-fold target encodings are fitted without the held-out month;
- matched-base round selection uses April/October rather than January/July;
- CatBoost and LOBT blend weights are fitted on the ten non-January/July months;
- current context features do not directly read departure targets or departure block times;
- strict time-window bounds exclude the queried departure from neighbour windows;
- submission generation validates row IDs, order, finiteness, schema, and `int32` output;
- rehearsal and submission use the shared `prc.submit.predict_frame()` path;
- current stored prediction artifacts have complete, unique movement-ID alignment;
- the robustness check is valuable as a concentration diagnostic;
- the leakage probe detects direct reads from the departure frame's withheld block-time column.

The rehearsal leakage probe is narrower than its prose suggests: both probe arms receive the same
already-blanked `movements` frame (`scripts/rehearse_submission.py:35-47`). It would not catch a
future feature reading a departure target or block time through `movements`. No current inspected
feature does so.

## Decision rules after this audit

Effective immediately, model claims should follow these rules:

1. Name the exact baseline, artifact, population, and model version for every RMSE or SSE claim.
2. Call the current stored chain a v29 approximation until exact production-equivalent nested OOF
   exists.
3. Treat January/July 2025 as calendar-matched selection diagnostics, not untouched holdouts.
4. Use whole-month LOMO for paired screening, not absolute prediction of the board score.
5. Do not scale local absolute RMSE to a hypothetical leaderboard score.
6. Do not use leaderboard transfer to set thresholds, priorities, blends, or confidence.
7. Require nested validation for second-stage models and calibrated mixtures.
8. Report row-level and airport-day/date-clustered uncertainty.
9. Use drop-row analysis as a concentration diagnostic, not an automatic veto.
10. Distinguish predeclared tiny-cohort mechanisms from post-hoc arbitrary-row gains.
11. Replace "irreducible" with "not improved by tested observables and model families" unless a
    formal information bound exists.
12. Do not use target-flight post-event external data without a temporal-availability ruling.
13. Do not use finalized daily external aggregates until their permissible prediction-time status
    is settled.
14. Preserve scorer result JSON, model metadata, input hashes, and generation commands for every
    future submission.

## Corrective work, in order

1. **Resolve ATFM temporal availability.** This affects the currently best model artifact.
2. **Build exact nested v29 OOF.** Include the ATFM-enabled base, both AOBT members, LOBT member,
   Rome layers, stacker, and every production clip/rule in production order.
3. **Re-evaluate the stacker under strict nesting.** Do not rely on its current 0.7-0.8 second OOF
   estimate.
4. **Add clustered uncertainty.** Resample airport-days or dates, with January and July reported
   separately.
5. **Separate selection and confirmation.** Predeclare candidate logic and reserve legal months or
   nested inner folds for threshold and architecture choices.
6. **Quarantine leaderboard-derived strategy.** Keep scores in `docs/submissions.md`, but remove
   their use as acceptance or prioritisation evidence.
7. **Resolve ADS-B prediction-time legality.** Only then perform a multi-date coverage census and
   decide whether geometry/context work is justified.
8. **Repair the current document map.** Add supersession notices to stale documents and update
   obsolete status text without erasing historical measurements.
9. **Create an artifact manifest.** Record source commit, command, inputs, hashes, feature flags,
   folds, and outputs for every production and OOF component.

## Final interpretation

The repository is not built on a wholly mistaken understanding. Its biggest gains came from valid
discoveries about timestamp semantics, NM clock availability, missing-record regimes, and the need
to retain extreme rows.

The main failure mode is stronger: tentative evidence repeatedly became categorical narrative.
Negative experiments became claims of irreducibility; approximate OOF became production anatomy;
calendar-matched selection months became a scored-population holdout; concentration diagnostics
became universal rejection rules; and leaderboard observations became strategic evidence despite
the stated policy.

Future work should preserve the verified data foundations while resetting confidence in the
current ceiling, v29 residual anatomy, small-gain estimates, external-data timing, and any claim
whose support depends on leaderboard transfer.
